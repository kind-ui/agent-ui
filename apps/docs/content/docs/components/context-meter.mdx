## Composition

```text
ContextMeter
├── ContextMeterTrigger
│   └── ContextMeterValue
└── ContextMeterContent
    ├── ContextMeterTitle
    ├── ContextMeterCompact
    ├── ContextMeterCompacting
    ├── ContextMeterDescription
    ├── ContextMeterBar
    ├── ContextMeterBreakdown
    ├── ContextMeterRows
    └── ContextMeterLimits
```

`ContextMeter` renders these parts when you pass no children: a group named by its `label`. `ContextMeterTrigger` is a button that opens the details, and `ContextMeterValue` holds UI SDK's Meter, drawn as a ring with the percentage beside it. `ContextMeterContent` is the popover with the details: `ContextMeterTitle` names it ("57% full"), `ContextMeterCompact` is the **Compact** button and `ContextMeterCompacting` takes its place while compacting, `ContextMeterDescription` reads the tokens used, `ContextMeterBar` and `ContextMeterBreakdown` show what fills the window, `ContextMeterRows` lists each count and the cost, and `ContextMeterLimits` lists the plan's limits. Each part shows only when its option is set.

## Usage

### The Value

Pass `used` and `total` in tokens. The ring fills to how much of the window is used and the percentage sits beside it, in tabular numbers at a fixed width, so a new value never moves what's next to the meter. Screen readers hear the button as "57,600 of 128,000 tokens, 45%". Numbers follow the page's `lang`, or the browser's language without one; pass `locale` to choose another.

### Levels

From 80%, as the percentage reads, the ring turns to the warning color with an exclamation mark inside it, and from 95% to the danger color with its middle filled. The root's `data-level` reads `normal`, `high` or `full` for your own styles.

### Details

Pass `usage` with any of `input`, `output`, `reasoning` and `cached`, and `cost` with its `currency` (`"USD"` unless you say), to list them in the details. Only what you pass shows, `0` included, in that order. Without either, the details show the title and the tokens used.

```tsx
<ContextMeter used={57_600} total={128_000} usage={{ input: 40_000, cached: 20_000 }} cost={0.42} currency="EUR" />
```

### Breakdown and Compact

Pass `breakdown`, a list of slices with an `id`, a `label` and a `value` in tokens, to show what fills the window: a stacked bar, then a row per slice with its tokens and share, then **Free space**. Slices mustn't overlap, so cached and reasoning counts go in `usage`, not here. Slices take their colors from a category palette in order, never a status color. A slice of 0 keeps its row, so the details keep their height.

Pass `compactAt`, from 0 to 1, to mark where the conversation compacts on the bar; from 80% full the description adds it ("72,900 of 128,000 tokens · compacts at 80%"). Pass `onCompact` to show **Compact**. While the promise it returns is pending, or while you pass `compacting` (as when the agent compacts on its own), "Compacting" and a running mark take its place. A rejection announces "Couldn't compact".

### Limits

Pass `limits` for the plan's usage windows, like a 5-hour and a weekly limit. Each has an `id`, a `label`, `used` and `total` (in `unit`: `"requests"` by default, `"tokens"`, `"credits"` or `"currency"` with its `currency`), and optionally `resetsAt`. Each limit is a row with a bar and when it resets ("Resets 5:00 PM, in 2 hr"); with `windowStartedAt` too, it says whether usage is "Ahead of pace" or "On pace". From 80% used a row leads with "Running low". Resets read in `timeZone`, else the page's, and update once a minute.

Once a limit reaches 80%, the ring shows the fullest such limit instead of the context, with its label before the percentage, and the button is named by it ("Weekly limit, 84% used"), so a near cap isn't hidden behind a quiet context ring.

### Inline

Pass `layout="inline"` to put the details in the page without the ring, as on a settings page. Each limit adds its amount ("124 of 200 requests", "$20.50 of $50.00"). Without `total`, the summary is left out and only the limits show.

### Where It Compacted

To show where a conversation compacted, add a Status row to the transcript at that point. It's a recipe, not a part:

```tsx
import { Status } from "@/components/ui/status";

<Status status="complete" label="Compacted the conversation · 64k → 12k tokens" />
```

### Opening

The details open when the pointer rests on the meter for 600 ms, when keyboard focus rests on it as long, and when it's pressed, by pointer, Enter or Space. They close on Escape, a press outside or focus leaving the meter, and a press on the meter closes them unless resting the pointer opened them. Details the pointer opened also close 300 ms after it leaves both the meter and the details. Opening them by pointer or keyboard leaves focus on the meter, since they hold nothing to press.

On small touch screens the details present as a sheet; pass `presentation` as `"popover"` or `"sheet"` to choose one. Pass `open` and `onOpenChange` to control them, or `defaultOpen` to start with them open. `onOpenChange` gets UI SDK Popover's reason, like `trigger-hover`, `trigger-press` or `escape-key`, or `trigger-focus` when keyboard focus opened them.

### Motion

The ring sweeps to a new value, the details rise and fade in, and the breakdown's and limits' bars grow once as they open. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, or with `motion="reduced"`, the ring moves at once and the details only fade, over 120 ms, and with `motion="none"` nothing animates.

### Agent

An agent connected with `createAgent` finds the meter by its label and reads whether its details are open. `open` is offered while they're closed and `close` while they're open; both report `details.source` as `"agent"` to `onOpenChange`. `compact` does what **Compact** does, offered while `onCompact` is set and nothing is compacting. Like any component with a button, it also has UI SDK's generic `press`.
