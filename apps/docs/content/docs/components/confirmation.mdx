Use a confirmation when the agent needs a person's go-ahead before it acts, like deleting a file or sending an email. It names the request, offers the choices the host supplies, then shows the recorded outcome in their place.

## Composition

```text
Confirmation
├── ConfirmationTitle
├── ConfirmationMeta
├── ConfirmationRisk
│   └── ConfirmationRiskIcon
├── ConfirmationReason
├── ConfirmationChoices
│   ├── ConfirmationCancel
│   ├── ConfirmationDeny
│   ├── ConfirmationAlways
│   ├── ConfirmationApprove
│   └── ConfirmationError
├── ConfirmationDetail
└── ConfirmationOutcome
    └── ConfirmationOutcomeIcon
```

`Confirmation` renders these parts when you pass no children. It's a group named by its `label`, which `ConfirmationTitle` shows above the choices and keeps above the outcome; inside a [Tool](./tool.mdx) the title is hidden, since the tool already names the request. Text in backticks shows as code, as in ``"Allow `search_docs`?"``. `ConfirmationMeta` sits at the title's end, `ConfirmationRisk` says what could go wrong, and `ConfirmationReason` is a text box for the reason Deny sends. The choices are native buttons, Deny first and Approve, the primary, last; `ConfirmationCancel` closes the reason box, and `ConfirmationError` says "Decision not sent. Try again." beside them. `ConfirmationDetail` says what Always allow covers. `ConfirmationOutcome` takes the choices' place once they're gone, with a status mark and where the request stands.

## Usage

### Requests

Pass the host's request as `approval`: an `approvalId`, a `generation` and a `state` of `requested`, `submitting`, `submit-failed`, `responded`, `auto`, `expired` or `cancelled`, with the `decision` once there is one. Pass `onDeny` to show **Deny** and `onApprove` to show **Approve**; only the choices you pass render. Each is called with the `approvalId` and `generation` it decides, and `details.source` says who decided, like `"mouse"` or `"agent"`.

```tsx
<Confirmation
  label="Run search_docs?"
  approval={approval}
  onApprove={(decision) => respond({ ...decision, approved: true })}
  onDeny={(decision) => respond({ ...decision, approved: false })}
/>
```

An `auto` request shows its outcome, like "Approved automatically", and no choices.

### Always Allow

Pass `onAlwaysAllow` to offer **Always allow** between Deny and Approve, for a person who doesn't want to be asked again. `alwaysTarget` says what it covers and shows in the button, `alwaysScope` says where, and `alwaysDetail` is a line under the choices. The decision carries `always: true` and the scope; record them in the approval's `decision`, and the outcome reads "Always allowed `search_docs` in this project". You keep the allow list, and the way to take it back.

When the choices don't fit one row, Always allow takes its own row above Deny and Approve, so Approve stays last.

### Risk and Deadlines

`risk="danger"` makes Approve a danger button and leaves out Always allow; `riskText` says why, after a warning mark. With `expiresAt` on the approval, the meta reads "Decide in 4 min", then in seconds under a minute. Past it the meta reads "Expired" and the choices stay until you report the request `expired`.

### Sending a Decision

A press disables every choice at once, so a second press, even on another choice, calls nothing. The pressed choice shows a running mark in place of its label, and the meta reads "Sending decision", while its call is pending. Return a promise to keep them disabled until it settles; they stay disabled after that until the host reports on the request, whether it's `submitting`, `submit-failed` or `responded`. A rejection, or `submit-failed`, makes them available again, and `submit-failed` shows "Decision not sent. Try again." beside them.

When the host replaces the request with a new `generation`, the choices are available again, and a late result for the old one changes nothing.

### Reasons

Pass `reason` to ask why before a denial. Approve stays one press. The first press on Deny opens a box above the choices and moves focus into it, without calling `onDeny`; while it's open, **Deny** sends its text as `decision.reason`, trimmed, and leaves it out when it's empty, and **Cancel** closes the box and returns focus to Deny. Approve is hidden while the box is open. It keeps its text through a new `generation` of the same request, and empties for a new `approvalId`. Once the decision is recorded, the box collapses and the outcome shows the reason after "Denied".

### Outcome

While the host sends a decision, the outcome reads "Sending decision". Once it's recorded, it reads "Approved", "Always allowed", "Approved automatically", "Denied" (with the reason), "Expired before a decision" or "Cancelled before a decision", each after its own mark: a check, a slash in a ring, a clock or a minus. It has the same height as the choices, so nothing above or below moves. If a choice had focus when it went away, focus moves to the outcome, or to the element `focusFallback` returns.

Screen readers hear "Decision not sent" when a decision fails, and the outcome when it's recorded.

### Motion

The choices and the outcome fade in when they swap, and the reason box expands as it opens and collapses as it closes. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, or with `motion="reduced"`, they fade over 120 ms and the box doesn't change height, and with `motion="none"` they swap at once.

### Agent

An agent connected with `createAgent` finds the confirmation by its label and reads its `approvalState`. It can approve, always allow or deny while those choices show, and `deny` takes a reason, which it sends at once without opening the reason box. Every decision waits for a person to confirm, unless the host's `allow` lets it through; this can't be turned off.
